# Zhaekoryvath

> An ivory compass points toward the wound of an unnamed star.

## The Direction Beneath North

The compass was discovered inside a loaf of bread baked during an eclipse. Its casing was ivory, its needle a sliver of black horn. There were no letters around its rim. Instead, four shallow impressions suggested that someone had pressed their fingertips into the surface while it was still soft.

The baker set it beside the window. The needle pointed toward a wall. He carried it into the street. The needle continued pointing toward the wall, although the wall was now behind him. He took it beyond the city, where the needle pointed toward the place his wall would have occupied if the city had followed him.

By evening, he understood that the compass did not recognize distance. He never established what it recognized instead. That night, a fifth impression appeared on its rim.

## A Map Drawn in Milk

The first map was poured onto a dark table. The milk formed a narrow coastline surrounding an inland sea shaped like an eyelid. Within the sea stood seven islands. None cast a reflection. At the center of the largest island, a pale mark resembled a tower viewed from beneath the ground.

The baker asked a sailor to read it. The sailor recognized every harbor and denied having visited any of them. When he touched the smallest island, his sleeve became heavy with rain. When he touched the largest, a door opened somewhere in the house, though the house contained no doors that had been closed.

They left the map overnight. In the morning, the milk had become a sheet of translucent stone. A tiny footprint crossed its coast. The footprint had six toes and no heel. The compass needle pointed directly into it.

The sailor took the stone away wrapped in a sail. The baker kept the compass. Neither remembered which of them had proposed the division.

## The Tower Inside the Footprint

Three winters later, a woman arrived carrying a ladder small enough to fit inside her mouth. She said the sailor had reached the tower, but had been unable to leave its upper room. There was a window in that room, and through the window he could see the bakery as it had looked before either man was born.

The woman placed her ladder beside the compass. Its rungs lengthened until they crossed the entire table. Between the rungs, the baker saw a stairway descending through clouds. At its foot stood a door covered in overlapping bronze scales.

He asked whether the sailor was alive. The woman said the question required a direction. He asked whether the sailor was north of death. She considered this carefully, then shook her head.

Before leaving, she took one of the compass's impressions onto her thumb. The ivory became smooth where her hand had rested. Afterward, no bread baked in that house would rise without a pinch of sea salt.

## What Holds the Door Closed

Behind the scaled door sleeps a creature whose name is longer than the tower's shadow. Its head rests beneath the inland sea. Its tail passes through every island without disturbing their soil. Its wings are folded around something that shines only when nobody remembers the sun.

Those who dream of the creature wake with a taste of copper. Those who dream of the door find a bronze scale beneath their pillows. The scales are warm until weighed. Once placed on a balance, they become cold and acquire the weight of whichever object is imagined most clearly.

The baker collected eleven scales. He laid them around the compass and imagined a feather. The table sank through the floor. From below came the sound of an enormous animal turning over in its sleep.

He imagined nothing afterward. The table returned, but its legs were wet with milk.

## The Missing Letter

A sentence appeared beneath the compass needle when the moon vanished behind a cloud. It could be read only in the brief interval before the cloud moved away:

*Zhae — kory — vath — ulenn.*

The baker copied it onto paper. His copy contained one more letter than the original. He made another copy and found one letter missing. A third attempt produced a drawing of the scaled door, viewed from the side that had never been opened.

He burned the papers separately. Their smoke formed three different constellations above the hearth. Each constellation contained the same dark gap. For the rest of that winter, the compass pointed toward the gap whenever a fire was lit.

The baker no longer asked where the needle led. He asked what might arrive if it stopped pointing.

## The Voyage Without Departure

The sailor returned on an afternoon when all the clocks in the city showed different years. He carried the stone map, now folded like cloth, and a small window wrapped in his coat. Through the window, the baker could see the upper room of the tower. Someone stood inside with their back turned.

The sailor placed the window beside the compass. The needle rose from its casing and hovered between them. For an instant, the ivory impressions resembled the prints of a claw too large to belong to anything outside a dream.

Neither man moved. The figure in the window lifted one hand. Beyond the city, something bright fell upward into the sky.

The compass has remained still ever since.

The window has remained open.

And every morning, before the bread is cut, someone listens for the sea inside the crust.
